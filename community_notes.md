# Milestone 1 — community and candidate distinctions

**Community:** [r/movies](https://www.reddit.com/r/movies/) — public, general film
discussion, not tied to one release. I'll skip the `Official Discussion [SPOILERS]`
threads when collecting so examples stay readable for anyone.

**What I read:** 100 recent public comments across threads like "The Best Use of
the F-Word in a PG-13 Movie", "How accurate is Taken (2008) to reality?", "What
movies were considered mediocre when released…", and the KPop Demon Hunters 2
release news.

## Candidate distinctions

**1. Some comments cite a specific, checkable fact. Most don't.**
A year, a number, a release history, a named person.

> "Frozen, Zootopia, Moana and Inside Out pushed out sequels 7-10 years later
> and each of those were huge successes."

> "there was no pg-13 yet. it's exactly why it was created."

vs. "It's fantasy. It's not a realistic portrayal of human trafficking at all."
(a claim, no support).

**2. Some answer the thread with just a title or a quote. Others name it *and*
say why.**

> "Home Alone"

> "Open the fucking door"

vs.

> "Warren in Empire Records … My name isn't fucking Warren! It's well placed,
> the actor delivers it well, and it gives voice to everyone else's frustration…"

**3. Some comments are sarcasm or a joke that says the opposite of what it
means.** These read like confident claims if you take them literally.

> "This movie is a documentary. Incredibly realistic and 100% believable."
> (on Taken)

> "Can confirm. I got kidnapped in Europe and Liam Neeson saved me"

**4. Some comments are pure feeling, with no claim at all.**

> "Hell yeah!"

> "God damn, that sounds cool."

## Also noticed

- Personal anecdotes used as evidence ("My dad's truck was in Rocky 2", the
  *Restoration* extra story). Could fold into 1 or 2, or be its own thing.
- A few comments are arguments with another user, not about movies at all.
  Probably exclude these from collection rather than label them.

# Milestone 2: stress test

Eight posts written by Claude to sit on the label boundaries, then classified
using the definitions in the README. "Shaky" means the first draft of the
definitions didn't settle it, so a rule was added.

| # | Post | Label | Clean? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Nolan shot Oppenheimer on IMAX 65mm and it shows. The Trinity test scene is the best thing he's done." | `analysis` | Clean: checkable fact backing a judgment |
| 2 | "Studios basically stopped making mid-budget dramas years ago, that's why everything is a franchise now." | `hot_take` | Shaky: sounds factual, names nothing. Led to the **specific, look-up-able** rule |
| 3 | "Heat (1995). Coffee shop scene. Nothing else comes close." | `hot_take` | Shaky: has a title and year, but those are what's being nominated, not support. Led to the **nominated thing isn't support** rule |
| 4 | "LMAO the Rock looking 60 in this poster" | `hot_take` | Shaky: jokey, but "looks 60" is a judgment. Humor alone doesn't make it `reaction` |
| 5 | "This made $1.4B worldwide and people still call it a flop lol" | `analysis` | Clean: a number backing an argument, casual tone doesn't matter |
| 6 | "Oh wow. Just got out of the theater. I need a minute." | `reaction` | Clean |
| 7 | "Yeah, because Taken is SO realistic." | `hot_take` | Shaky: sarcasm. Led to the **label by what it means** rule |
| 8 | "Saw it opening night in 2010 and the whole theater gasped at the hallway fight." | `reaction` | Shaky: has a date, but it's a memory, not support for a claim. Led to the **anecdote** rule |

All 8 classify cleanly with the added rules. `hot_take` will be the biggest
bucket in r/movies, so watch the 70% cap in Milestone 4.
